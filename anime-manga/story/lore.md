# Lore — CARTHAGE SAGA

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Backstory: it constrains the present and is not necessarily known,
or known accurately, to the characters. That gap is the point of this file.

## Deep history

`[A]` The Canaanite coastal cities survive the Bronze Age collapse by going to sea. Tyre, on its
island, becomes the richest of them: cedar, purple, silver, and the carrying trade. From the ninth
century the Assyrian empire takes tribute, then more than tribute; Sidon is destroyed and its people
enslaved by Esarhaddon. Tyre pays and endures.

`[B]` The westward colonies — Utica, Gadir, Lixus, the Sardinian and Sicilian anchorages — are
built by a city under pressure, as much escape valve as enterprise. **Carthage is founded by people
running from something.** Every later Carthaginian generation forgets this, and the series keeps
reminding the audience.

## The founding event

`[C]` **The flight of Elissa**, c. 825 BC. See the dossier, Part Two, for the legend in full and
for what is defensible in it. The series dramatises the **reconstruction** — a losing faction in a
Tyrian succession struggle, led by the dead high priest's widow, buying land from Libyan chiefs
with temple silver — and then shows the **legend being invented** across ch. 19–22, in the mouths of
people who were present and have reasons to lie.

The three fixed elements the story keeps `[C]`:
1. A royal brother, **Pygmalion**, who kills the priest **Acerbas/Zakarbaal** for his treasure.
2. A **sea-trick with sacks** that makes the crew unable to go home.
3. A **death by fire**, freely chosen, which the city will retell for six hundred years and get wrong.

Everything else in Book One is `[B]` reconstruction or `[F]` invention.

## Myths, religions, legends — which are true, distorted, false

| Belief | Held by | Verdict on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Elissa bought the hill with an ox-hide cut into a thread | Every Carthaginian, from ch. 21 onward | **Distorted `[C]`.** A Greek pun on *byrsa*. The series shows the real transaction — a hard-bargained annual rent — and then shows the story being improved in the telling, twice, one generation apart |
| Elissa burned herself rather than marry the Libyan king Iarbas | Carthage | **Distorted.** She dies by fire; the series' reason differs and is shown; the Iarbas version is composed afterwards by a priest who needs it to mean something |
| Carthage owes a tithe to Melqart of Tyre for ever | Carthage; Tyre | **True `[A]`.** Still being paid centuries later. Becomes a political weapon in ch. 27 |
| Aeneas and Dido | Romans, from the first century BC | **False `[C]`, and anachronistic by eleven hundred years.** Cannot appear in this series except as a joke the audience cannot hear. Not used |
| The city can buy anything, including the favour of the gods | Carthaginian elites | **The series' central error.** Argued from ch. 21 to ch. 200 |
| Carthaginians burn their children | Greeks and Romans, universally | **Disputed `[C]`.** Staged as rare, elite, crisis-driven, and contested *by Carthaginians on screen*. The Diodorus account of 310 BC is dramatised explicitly as a Greek claim, in a scene where a Carthaginian denies it and the audience is not told who is lying |
| Hannibal swore eternal hatred of Rome at nine years old | Everyone, including Hannibal | **True that he swore `[A]`; disputed what it meant `[C]`.** The series shows the scene in ch. 116 and re-shows it, differently remembered, three times: by Hamilcar's officers, by Hannibal at thirty, and by Hannibal at sixty-four |
| Rome is a nation of honourable men who keep their word | Rome | **Complicated `[A]`.** Rome keeps treaties meticulously and takes Sardinia anyway. Both facts are shown |
| *Punica fides* — Carthaginian faithlessness | Rome | **Propaganda `[A]`, with a grain in it.** The series lets a Roman character make the case convincingly, and then lets a Carthaginian answer with Sardinia |

## Organisations and their origins

| Body | Origin | `Tier` |
|---|---|---|
| The **suffeteship** | Evolves out of the colony's council of leading families; kingship fades rather than falls | `[B]` |
| The **Council of 104** | Created after the Magonid collapse (post-480 BC) to stop any house monopolising war again — i.e. **it exists because of Himera**, which the audience has watched | `[B]` |
| The **Sacred Band** | 4th century; the citizen elite's answer to Greek hoplites. Destroyed at the Crimissus, 339 | `[A]` |
| The **mercenary system** | Grows because citizens are more valuable as rowers, traders and farmers than as spearmen. Rational, profitable, and in 241 BC nearly fatal | `[B]` |
| The **Barcid army in Iberia** | 237 BC onward: the first Carthaginian army loyal to a family rather than to the city — which is exactly what the Hundred and Four was created to prevent | `[A]`/`[B]` |
| The **Knot-keepers' guild-family** | Rope and cordage for the cothon; one of the hereditary harbour trades | `[F]` |

## Artifacts, bloodlines, relics

- **The knotted cord** `[F]` — the Knot-keepers' family record. One knot per generation, carried from
  Tyre. Read like a rosary. The series' single continuous physical object.
- **The temple tithe chest** `[A]`/`[B]` — the annual shipment to Melqart at Tyre; a recurring
  establishing image, and in ch. 27 a hostage.
- **The Nora Stone** `[A]` — a real 9th-century Phoenician inscription from Sardinia, read by many
  as naming Pygmalion. Appears once, in ch. 31, being carved: the audience knows what it is and the
  characters do not.
- **Hanno's *Periplus*** `[A]` — the inscription set up in the temple of Baal Hammon on his return,
  Ch. 41. The audience is told, in narration, that this is the only Carthaginian book that will
  survive, and only in a Greek translation.
- **Mago's twenty-eight books** `[A]` — written on screen in ch. 49 by a retired general who would
  rather talk about manure than politics. Narration notes that Rome will translate them after
  burning everything else.
- **The bronze tablets at Croton** `[A]` — Hannibal's own campaign record in Punic and Greek,
  set up in 203 BC. Polybius reads them sixty years later. This is *why the series can be made at all*,
  and the chapter says so.
- **The Barcid line** `[A]` — Hamilcar; Hannibal, Hasdrubal, Mago; the unnamed daughters, one of whom
  marries Hasdrubal the Fair and one the Numidian **Naravas**. The women are unnamed in the sources;
  the series names them `[F]` and flags it.

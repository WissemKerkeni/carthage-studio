# LOC-01 — TYRE

## Identity

- **ID:** LOC-01
- **Name:** Tyre / Ṣūr `[A]` — "the rock". Characters say *Sur*; narration says Tyre
- **Type:** exterior city, island
- **Region:** the Levantine coast, c. 825 BC
- **First appearance:** ch. 1 · **Returns:** ch. 12 (the tithe), ch. 25, ch. 52–82 (destroyed), ch. 180
- **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Why this location matters.** Tyre is the thing Carthage is running from and the thing it spends
> six hundred years trying to be. It is shown exactly twice as a living city — ch. 1 and ch. 25 —
> and then as a crucifixion ground in ch. 52. The audience must be able to recognise it instantly
> in all three states, so its establishing angles are fixed here.

## Canonical description

An island half a mile off its own mainland, so crowded that the buildings have grown upward into
five and six storeys of mudbrick and cedar, leaning over streets that never get sun. Two harbours:
the Sidonian to the north, the Egyptian to the south, both packed. The whole island smells of
rotting shellfish, because the murex vats are on the windward shore and the wind does not cooperate.
It is the richest place in the world and it is filthy, loud, and frightened — Assyrian tribute
officers have desks in the counting houses, and everyone knows what Esarhaddon did to Sidon.

## Architecture & materials `[A]`/`[B]`

Mudbrick over stone footings; cedar beams, imported and expensive, used as a status display in
lintels and upper floors. Flat roofs used as living space — laundry, sleeping in summer, gossip.
Harbour works in dressed ashlar, the only monumental masonry in the city. The temple of Melqart on
the high ground, its precinct walled, its two bronze pillars the one thing visible from the sea.
Nothing is new. Everything has been repaired.

Colour, for the anime phase: bone and dust, with the murex shore staining everything downwind a
dirty purple-brown that is emphatically **not** the Tyrian purple of the finished dye.

## Layout — fixed

Blocking depends on this staying constant.

1. **The temple precinct**, highest point, north-centre. Zakarbaal's ground.
2. **The Sidonian harbour**, north — naval and official. The tribute ships load here.
3. **The Egyptian harbour**, south — commercial, dirtier, where the escape sails from in ch. 4.
4. **The murex shore**, west/windward — vats, crushing yards, the smell.
5. **The rope walk**, running four hundred paces from the harbour wall to the timber yard `[F]`,
   between the Egyptian harbour and the timber yard, in the long shadow of the temple.
   **Chapter 1 lives here.**
6. **The counting houses**, along the Sidonian harbour front, where the Assyrians sit.
7. **The palace**, east, facing the mainland. Pygmalion's. Shown only from outside.

## The rope walk — chapter 1's set `[F]`

A straight open lane, four hundred paces, sand floor, low wall on the seaward side. Spinning posts
at the north end; the laying machine and the tar pot at the south. Workers walk **backwards** down
the lane with hemp round their waists, paying out yarn as they go. Hemp dust hangs in the light.
The temple's shadow crosses the lane at a diagonal and moves through the chapter, which is how the
reader is told that time is passing in a chapter with no events.

**This diagonal shadow is the chapter's clock and is continuity.** Establish it in panel 1 and move
it consistently.

## Lighting

- **Default key:** hard, high, from the left (series default). The rope walk runs roughly north–south, so the temple shadow falls across it from the east.
- **Day:** blown-out white sand, black shadow, almost no midtone. Heat as white.
- **Night:** ch. 4 only. Black gutters, lamp-lit faces, no moon — the text specifies a moonless night.
- **Mood variant:** ch. 52, Tyre destroyed. Same angles, same pillars, crosses along the shore.

## Atmosphere

The smell first, always — the series' opening line is about it. Constant noise: hammering from the
yards, gulls, the harbour, and human shouting from six floors up. Crowd density is high everywhere
except the rope walk, which is deliberately empty and quiet, because rope needs length and nobody
else wants to be near the tar. **Zakarbaal chooses it for exactly that reason**, and the reader
should be able to work that out without being told.

## Sightlines / establishing angles

Fixed. Tyre is only ever established from these three.

1. **From the sea, low** — the island as a wall of leaning buildings, the two bronze pillars above them. The series' signature Tyre image; used in ch. 1, ch. 25, and ch. 52.
2. **Down the rope walk, south to north** — a four-hundred-pace one-point perspective with the temple at the far end, workers receding. The chapter 1 hero angle.
3. **From the temple steps, looking down** — the whole crowded island and both harbours, tribute ships visible. Used when someone powerful is looking at the city as a possession.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Six storeys, leaning.** Tyre is vertical and crowded. It is never drawn as a low classical town.
2. **The two bronze pillars are always visible** from any seaward angle. They are how the reader knows this is Tyre.
3. **The tribute ships are always in the background.** Tyre is never shown as free.
4. **The rope walk is empty and quiet.** It is the one calm space in a loud city.
5. **No Greek or Roman architecture.** No columns of classical order, no pediments, no white marble.

# Chapter 04 — SACKS

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Source: `THE-STORY.md` §4 · `arcs.md` Arc 1, marked ★.
Book One · Arc 1 · 28 pages. **The arc's first set piece.**

## Role in the story

The series' first statement of method, and the scene the whole show rhymes against.

**Elissa does not inspire loyalty. She manufactures it by removing the alternative.** Forty sacks
of sand go over the rail in front of the king's pursuit, every man aboard is seen to have destroyed
the royal treasury, and there is no longer any such place as home.

The source's production note is binding: *Hannibal will do a version of this in chapter 137,
exchanging Iberian and African troops across the sea so that every soldier's family is a hostage.
The rhyme is the point.* **Compose this chapter knowing ch. 137 will quote it.**

## Summary

Eight ships, a hundred and forty people, a night with no moon. Pursuit lights on the horizon off
Cyprus. Elissa has the sacks brought up — heavy, corded, sealed, and everyone aboard watched them
loaded and believes they hold the temple silver. She speaks a prayer for her husband and has them
thrown into the sea, forty of them, one after another.

Then she tells them what was in the sacks. Sand. The silver has been in the bilge under the ballast
since the second night.

A sailor begins to laugh. It is not a good laugh. Elishat, fourteen, holding a stay, watches a
hundred and forty people become a single thing.

## Opens on

The ballast. A held close-up of stones in the bilge, in the dark, with water moving.
**The audience is shown the answer in the first panel and does not know it.**

## Ends on

Elishat's face, holding a stay, in the dark, listening to a man laugh.

## Reader takeaway

1. The silver was never in the sacks.
2. Every person on those ships is now a thief who destroyed the evidence, in front of witnesses.
3. Elissa did this deliberately, and it worked.

## Information withheld

- Nothing, by the end. **This is the one chapter in the arc that pays off completely inside itself**, which is why it can be the set piece.

## Structure — the four movements

| Movement | Pages | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The ballast | 1–3 | The bilge. Stones. Water. No context |
| 2. The pursuit | 4–10 | Lights on the horizon. The ships running. Fear established without a word spoken about it |
| 3. **The sacks** | 11–22 | Brought up, the prayer, and forty of them going over, one after another |
| 4. The turn | 23–28 | *Sand.* The understanding spreading across a deck. The laugh. Elishat's face |

**Movement 3 is twelve pages for one repeated action.** That repetition is the chapter — the
audience must feel forty fortunes go under the water, which means seeing enough of them to lose
count. Vary scale and angle; never vary the action.

## The spread

`manga-style.md` allots Book One exactly two spreads. **This chapter has one:** the sacks going over
the rail, pp. 16–17, roughly the eighth sack — late enough that the reader has settled into the
rhythm, early enough that there is more to come.

Bleed permitted (`style-guide.md` §9: reserved for the sea).

## The purple

Colour-arc step 3 of 5. **First visible degradation** — salt, wet, and three days in the same
clothes. In black and white the robe's dense black begins to break up into hatching.

**28 pages.** The arc's second-largest chapter after ch. 8.

## Craft notes

- **Black gutters throughout** (`style-guide.md` §9: black gutter = the sea at night). The chapter is the series' first sustained use, and it should be unmissable after twenty-two pages of white gutters in ch. 2 and ch. 3.
- **No SFX on the sacks entering the water.** The series has very few sound effects and spending them here would make the scene operatic. Silence, and the reader supplies it.
- **The prayer is not translated.** Elissa speaks it for her husband; the panels show the crew's faces, not subtitles. What she says is between her and a dead man.
- **Elishat has no dialogue in this chapter.** Not one line. She holds a stay and watches, and the chapter's last page belongs to her.
- The laugh: **one panel, no sound effect, no onomatopoeia.** A man's open mouth in the dark. `style-guide.md` §4 permits the full open-mouthed shout a handful of times per Book — this is one of them.

# Arc 1 — THE FLIGHT FROM TYRE (ch. 1–10)

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Season One · Book One · chapters 1–10.
Source: `story/THE-STORY.md` §1–§10–14 · `story/arcs.md` Book One, Arc 1.

---

## What the arc is for

Ten chapters to get a hundred and forty frightened people from a rope walk in Tyre to a rented hill
in Africa, and to establish — before the series has a city, a war or a protagonist — **the four
debts Carthage never pays off**:

| Debt | Incurred in | Collected in |
|---|---|---|
| The stolen treasure | ch. 3–4 | Never. It becomes the city's founding capital and its founding crime |
| The first office sold | ch. 5 | Every Carthaginian election for six hundred years (`world.md`, Aristotle's complaint) |
| The eighty women | ch. 6 | Never acknowledged by Carthage, and never dropped by the series |
| **The rent** | ch. 8 | Book Two, ch. 28, when Mago stops paying it — and in every Libyan revolt thereafter |

If the audience leaves Arc 1 understanding those four things, the remaining 190 chapters work.

## The arc's shape

A flat, quiet opening (ch. 1–2), one off-screen murder that starts everything (ch. 3), the series'
first set piece (ch. 4), then three chapters of the cost of survival (ch. 5–7), the negotiation that
is the whole point (ch. 8), and two chapters of aftermath that end with a knot in a piece of rope
(ch. 9–10).

**No battle. No villain on screen.** Pygmalion is never shown; the antagonist of Arc 1 is the
situation.

## An ordering conflict to resolve

**`arcs.md` places The First Knot at ch. 9 and Winter at ch. 10. `THE-STORY.md` says the knot is
tied *in the spring, after* the first winter:** *"The first winter nearly finished them… In the
spring, Elishat's father tied a knot in a length of ship's cable and hung it on the wall."*

As numbered, the arc shows the commemoration before the thing being commemorated.

Three ways out, `PROPOSED`:

1. **Swap them** — ch. 9 Winter ◆, ch. 10 The First Knot. Chronological, and gives the arc a
   quieter, better final image. **Recommended.**
2. **Keep the order and re-motivate ch. 9** — the first knot records surviving *the voyage and the
   landing*, not the winter; ch. 10 then ends on the settlement's worst moment, and the second knot
   idea moves to Arc 2. Darker ending, and it costs the arc its resolution.
3. **Merge into one chapter** (ch. 9–10, 30 pages), winter into spring, ending on the knot.

This is a canon question, not a production one. **Chapters 9 and 10 below are written to option 1**
and both files say so at the top. Say the word and they swap back in five minutes.

## Craft rules specific to this arc

1. **Pygmalion is never shown.** He is a name, a rumour and a set of pursuit lights on a horizon. The arc's threat is entirely off screen.
2. **The purple fades.** `CHR-03`: Elissa's Tyrian robe degrades chapter by chapter from ch. 2 to ch. 8, and is worn again, grey-pink, at the pyre in ch. 18. Track it per chapter; it is Book One's only colour arc and it must be legible in black and white as a **tone value**, not a colour.
3. **The hand vocabulary.** Book One distinguishes four pairs of hands: Elishat's rope hands, Yatonbaal's horn callus, Zakarbaal's soft priest's hands, Bititt's laundry hands. Established in ch. 1 and ch. 6; used for the rest of the Book.
4. **Interpreters are characters** (`story-bible.md` §9.9). Chapter 8 runs through two of them plus a trader who is cheating everyone, and the *mistranslation is the content*, not colour.
5. **No aftermath chapter may be cut** (§9.5). Ch. 10 is marked ◆.
6. **Nobody says "Carthage."** The name is not coined until ch. 11. Across all of Arc 1 the settlement has no name, and the script never slips.

## Production order

Arc 1 is not produced in chapter order. Recommended:

1. **Finish the four missing character files** — CHR-13 Tikat first.
2. **LOC-18** (the bay and hill), then **LOC-16** (the ships). Both are reused for four chapters each and both block more pages than any character does.
3. Write **ch. 8's** script and storyboard next, not ch. 2 — it is the arc's thesis and the hardest chapter to get right. If ch. 8 works, the arc works.
4. Then ch. 4 and ch. 6, the two set pieces.
5. Then the connective chapters, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, which are comparatively straightforward.

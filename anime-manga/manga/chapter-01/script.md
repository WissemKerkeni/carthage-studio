# Chapter 1 — Script

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Derived from `story/THE-STORY.md` §1. 42 pages, 5 scenes.

**Dialogue provenance.** The source supplies four lines of dialogue and the narration block.
Those are marked `CANON-SOURCE` and **their wording is not to be altered**. Everything else is
marked `PROPOSED +` and exists because a 42-page chapter cannot be carried by four lines. Any
`PROPOSED +` line can be cut without damaging the chapter.

**Language coding** (`visual/style-guide.md` §10): Punic is unmarked. Sosylos narrates in Greek but
his narration is unmarked, because it is the series' voice rather than a scene.

---

## SCENE 0 — A ROOM IN BITHYNIA — SIX HUNDRED YEARS LATER — DAY
**Pages 1–4 · Purpose:** establish the frame and the loss before establishing anything else.
**Present:** Sosylos (CHR-06), face never shown.
**Ends when:** the voice says the word *ground*.

An old man's hands on a desk. A scroll, weighted at both ends. A window with nothing outside it
worth looking at. Dust in the light. The room is deliberately featureless — the reader must not be
able to date or place it.

> **NARRATION (CANON-SOURCE):**
> I am Sosylos, of Sparta, and I taught a boy Greek.
>
> He is dead now, and so is his city, and so is nearly everyone in what follows. I have written
> seven books about him. I expect that none of them will survive, because the people who will
> decide what survives are the people he spent his life fighting, and they write very well.
>
> So let me begin before him. Let me begin where he would have begun, because he was a man who
> never explained a battle without first explaining the ground.
>
> The ground is this: a city that was founded by people running for their lives, on land it did
> not own, with money it had stolen from a corpse.

**Direction.** No face. No reverse angle. The hands stop moving on *none of them will survive*, and
start again on *So let me begin*. Nothing else in the scene moves.

## SCENE 1 — TYRE, FROM THE SEA, THEN THE CITY — MORNING
**Pages 5–12 · Purpose:** the smell of the city.
**Present:** no named characters. Crowd only.
**Ends when:** the camera finds the rope walk and the noise drops out.

Hard cut from dust in a still room to the island: a wall of leaning six-storey buildings, the two
bronze pillars of Melqart above them, both harbours packed. Then down into it — the murex shore,
the vats, the shell mounds higher than a man, crushing yards, flies. The counting houses along the
Sidonian front, and among the clerks, men who are not Tyrian, with desks and tablets. Tribute ships
at the quay. Nobody looks at them.

This sequence is wordless except for one narration box, placed late, on the turn to page 9.

> **NARRATION (`PROPOSED +`, adapted from the source's first line):**
> Tyre smelled of dead shellfish, and everybody who lived there had stopped noticing.

**Direction.** Crowd density is maximal here — it is the contrast that makes the rope walk read as
empty. No character is favoured by framing. The camera is a visitor.

## SCENE 2 — THE ROPE WALK — LATE MORNING
**Pages 13–24 · Purpose:** the trade, in working detail; and Elishat.
**Present:** Elishat (CHR-12), Abdeshmun (CHR-34), 4–6 workers.
**Ends when:** a shadow falls across the lane from the north end.

Four hundred paces of open sand lane, low wall on the seaward side, spinning posts at one end and
the tar pot at the other. Hemp dust hanging in the light. The temple's shadow lies across the lane
on a diagonal. After the harbour, the silence should feel like pressure.

The work, shown properly and at length: hemp combed; yarn spun; the walkers pacing **backwards**
down the lane with the cord round their waists, paying out; yarn laid into strand; strand into
cable; the top cart travelling; tar.

Elishat is introduced **by her hands, before her face** — a splice, in close-up, done fast. Then a
wide shot that shows how small she is compared to the work.

> **NARRATION (`PROPOSED +`, adapted from the source):**
> Elishat was fourteen and could splice better than her father.
> This was not a matter of pride. It was a matter of small hands.

> **ABDESHMUN** (`PROPOSED +`, without looking up): Short on the third strand.
> **ELISHAT** (`PROPOSED +`): I know.

*(These two lines are the only dialogue in the scene and establish the relationship the character
files specify: he corrects, she has already seen it, neither remarks on it.)*

**Direction.** This is twelve pages of people working and it must never feel like filler. It reads
because the process is genuinely interesting and is shown with total specificity. Vary scale
aggressively — the lane in one-point perspective, then a thumb against a strand. **At least two
fully wordless pages.**

## SCENE 3 — THE ROPE WALK — MIDDAY
**Pages 25–38 · Purpose:** something is being prepared in secret.
**Present:** Zakarbaal (CHR-33), Abdeshmun, Elishat, workers.
**Ends when:** Zakarbaal leaves the lane.

A figure at the north end of the lane, in silhouette against the harbour light. He comes down the
rope walk alone.

**The first strange thing is that there is no one with him**, and the chapter must make the reader
feel that before anyone says it. The workers notice. Nobody stops working.

He stops. He lifts the hem of a very good robe clear of the tar. And he watches them work for the
time it takes to lay ten paces of cable — which the chapter shows in real time, in silence, across
a page turn, because the length of that silence is the whole scene.

Elishat watches his hands. Soft. No callus. A stylus mark. A burn across the palm.

> **ZAKARBAAL (CANON-SOURCE):** How much rope can you make in a month, and can you do it without anyone knowing?

Abdeshmun does not look up. The narration observes why.

> **NARRATION (`PROPOSED +`, adapted from the source):**
> In Tyre, that was the polite answer to a question like that.

> **ABDESHMUN (CANON-SOURCE):** That depends on what you tell the harbour master.
>
> **ZAKARBAAL (CANON-SOURCE):** Nothing. I intend to tell him nothing at all.

Held beat. The work does not stop. Nobody looks at anybody.

> **ABDESHMUN** (`PROPOSED +`, after a long moment, still working): How much do you want?

**No answer is shown.** Cut. Zakarbaal walking back up the lane, smaller each panel.

**Direction.** The 180-degree line is set the moment Zakarbaal stops and is not broken in this
chapter (`visual/manga-style.md`). The page-turn rule applies to the second canon line: *"Nothing.
I intend to tell him nothing at all."* lands on the first panel after a turn, and nothing is
revealed at the bottom of the preceding page.

## SCENE 4 — THE ROPE WALK — AFTERNOON
**Pages 39–42 · Purpose:** end on the rope.
**Present:** Elishat, Abdeshmun, workers.
**Ends when:** the page ends.

The lane, continuing. The shadow has moved. The work goes on and there is more of it than there was
this morning — more hemp in the yard than the panel on page 14 had.

Elishat looks up the empty lane once, then back at her hands.

Close on the laying: yarn into strand, strand into cable, turning. Hold. Hold. Out.

**No dialogue. No narration. No sound effect.**

**Direction.** The final page is a single panel or at most two. The chapter ends on the rope, and
the rope is doing exactly what it was doing on page 13 — except that the reader now cannot watch it
innocently. Nothing is explained. Do not add a closing narration box; the temptation will be strong
and it would wreck the chapter.

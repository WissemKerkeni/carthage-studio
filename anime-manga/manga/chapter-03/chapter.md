# Chapter 03 — GOLD UNDER THE FLOOR

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Source: `THE-STORY.md` §2–3 · `arcs.md` Arc 1, marked ★.
Book One · Arc 1 · 20 pages.

## Role in the story

The murder that starts six hundred years of history, and **it is not shown.**

The chapter is one room, one conversation, and one woman working out — while a man is still
speaking — that her brother paid him to be gentle with her. Everything the series is going to be
about its own evidence is in this chapter: the audience never sees the killing, only the accounting
that follows it.

This is the shortest chapter in the arc and the most tightly controlled.

## Summary

A chamberlain comes to tell Elissa that her husband is dead. He has been given money to break it
gently, and he breaks it gently. He is not cruel and he is not a conspirator; he is a man doing a
paid errand kindly, which is worse.

She does not weep in front of him. Later, in the story Carthage told about her, she wept
magnificently — and the series will show that story being composed, in ch. 21.

She says four words, and they are about the ships.

## Opens on

The chamberlain's feet, stopping outside a door. He waits before knocking — **three panels of a man
composing his face.** The audience does not yet know what he is there to say.

## Ends on

Her four words. Then out, immediately. No reaction shot held past the line.

## Reader takeaway

1. Zakarbaal is dead and Pygmalion did it for money that was owed, not for greed alone.
2. Elissa understood who paid the messenger **while he was still talking**, and gave no sign.
3. She is already thinking about ships.

## Information withheld

- The murder itself. **Never shown, in this chapter or in any later flashback.** This is a standing rule for the series.
- Where the treasure is. The audience does not learn about the bilge until ch. 4, at the same moment the crew does.
- Any confirmation that Pygmalion ordered it. It is an inference — hers, and the reader's.

## The craft problem: how she works it out

The chapter's whole value is that the reader solves it at the same speed she does, from staged
evidence rather than narration:

| Panel evidence | What it tells her |
|---|---|
| The chamberlain's sandals are new | `PROPOSED` |
| He has clearly rehearsed the opening sentence and then abandons it | `PROPOSED` |
| He says *"the king wishes you to know"* one beat too early | `PROPOSED` |
| He cannot say what Zakarbaal died of | `PROPOSED` |
| He does not sit down, and is not asked to | `PROPOSED` |

**She never states the conclusion.** The reader assembles it. If a test reader misses it, the fix is
sharper staging, not an added line.

## Her four words

The source specifies: *she says four words, and they are about the ships.* The exact wording is
**`UNCONFIRMED` and is the author's to write.** It is the first thing Elissa ever says on the page
and it sets her register for eighteen chapters.

Three `PROPOSED` options, offered only so the decision is concrete:

- *"How many ships sail?"*
- *"Which ships are ours?"*
- *"Ready the eight ships."*

The first two keep her asking questions she already knows the answers to — consistent with ch. 2's
introduction. The third commits her, which may be too early. **Author's call; nothing is written
into the script until it is made.**

## The purple

Colour-arc step 2 of 5. Still saturated, but she is **indoors and lamp-lit**, so the robe reads
darker and heavier than in ch. 2. First visible degradation is ch. 4.

## Page budget

**20 pages.** Roughly: 3 (the door) + 5 (the telling) + 6 (the evidence, staged) + 4 (her stillness)
+ 2 (the four words, and out).

## Craft notes

- **One room, twenty pages, two people.** Panel scale carries everything. The chapter should get tighter as it proceeds — start at medium, end on extreme close-up.
- **She does not cry, and the camera does not look for tears.** No trembling hands, no clenched jaw. `style-guide.md` §4: grief is a face that does not move.
- The chapter is a matched pair with **ch. 21**, where the same event is retold as melodrama by a priest. Stage this one so that the retelling in ch. 21 is recognisably the same scene, lied about. **Compose ch. 3 and ch. 21 together** when ch. 21 is written.

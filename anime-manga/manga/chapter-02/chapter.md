# Chapter 02 — THE SECOND MAN

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Source: `THE-STORY.md` §2–3 · `arcs.md` Arc 1.
Book One · Arc 1 · 22 pages.

## Role in the story

Chapter 1 showed a man preparing something in secret. This chapter says what, and why, **without a
single expository speech** — which is the entire craft problem of the chapter.

The politics are genuinely complicated: a dead king, a young son, a daughter married to the high
priest, temple money and palace money, and an Assyrian tribute that comes out of whichever purse is
weaker that year. The audience needs all of it and must not feel taught.

**The solution: play it as an accounting dispute.** Nobody explains the constitution. Two people
argue about who pays this year's tribute, in a room with ledgers, and the audience assembles the
politics themselves. Money is the series' native language from here on (`story-bible.md` §9.7).

## Summary

Zakarbaal is preparing an escape, badly, because he is a careful man who has never been frightened
and does not know how it is done. We see how badly: the rope order from ch. 1 is one of several
visible loose ends. Elissa is introduced doing arithmetic faster than the men in the room and saying
the result last, so that someone else proposes it.

The temple is refusing to fund the tribute. The king cannot be king without it. Neither of them
says out loud what that means, because both of them already know.

## Opens on

Ledgers. A tally of what the temple paid last year and what the palace paid, side by side, in
someone's hands. **The first image of chapter 2 is a column of figures**, and the series is telling
the audience what it is going to be about.

## Ends on

Zakarbaal alone, checking a list of things that are not ready. The audience sees at least two of
them are impossible. He does not.

## Reader takeaway

1. Elissa is the most capable person in any room she is in, and has learned to hide it.
2. The temple and the crown are the same family and different purses, and the tribute is the crack.
3. Zakarbaal's plan will not survive contact with anything.

## Information withheld

- That Zakarbaal will be dead within one chapter. **No foreshadowing.** He is not given a wistful last look at anything.
- That there will be a voyage, a city, or a founding.

## Elissa's introduction

She gets four pages. The sequence, `PROPOSED`:

1. She is in the room before the reader notices her — background of a wide shot, two pages earlier, unremarked.
2. The men argue a figure. Cut to her face. She has already finished the sum.
3. She says nothing for a full page while the men arrive at the wrong number.
4. She offers a correction as a question, to the least important man present, who then proposes it as his own.

**No heroic framing, no beauty pass** (`CHR-03` do-not-drift §3). Her authority is established by
arithmetic and timing, not by staging.

## The purple

Elissa's Tyrian robe is at **full saturation** here — the only fully saturated thing in the chapter
and, per `CHR-03`, the only purple in Book One. In black and white it reads as the **densest black
in the frame**. Every later chapter degrades it one step. Colour-arc step 1 of 5.

## Page budget

**22 pages.** Roughly: 4 (ledgers, the tribute problem) + 6 (the temple, Zakarbaal's preparations) +
4 (Elissa's introduction) + 6 (the accounting argument) + 2 (Zakarbaal's list).

## Craft notes

- **Interiors after chapter 1's exteriors.** Ch. 1 is sun, dust and white; ch. 2 is lamp-lit black. Heavy tone, deep shadow, `style-guide.md` §6: cold and fear are drawn as black.
- **Four walls, no establishing crutch.** After ch. 1's spread, this chapter is claustrophobic on purpose.
- Elissa's line in §3 — *she says four words, and they are about the ships* — belongs to **ch. 3**, not here. Do not use it early.

## Open questions

1. **Does the Assyrian tribute officer get a face?** Ch. 1 kept him a background shape. Giving him one scene here makes Tyre's fear concrete. Proposal: yes, one panel, no dialogue, never named.
2. **How much of the marriage do we see?** The source gives one scene between Zakarbaal and Elissa. Proposal: keep it to one, and make it about work rather than affection — they are colleagues, and the arc is stronger if the audience has to infer the rest.

## Role in the story

**The smallest chapter in the arc, and the one the entire series ends on.**

A tired man ties a knot in a piece of ship's cable, hangs it on a wall, and says six words. That
object — one knot per generation — is the only thing present in both the first chapter and the
last. In ch. 184 the audience is told it burned.

Arc 1 has spent nine chapters on a theft, a murder, a manufactured loyalty, a sold office, eighty
women taken off a beach, a tenancy agreement and a bad well. It ends on a length of rope and a
domestic exchange, because that is the register the series wants to be trusted in.

## Summary

Spring. The settlement has survived a winter that killed three. Elishat's father takes a length of
ship's cable, ties a single knot in it, and hangs it on the wall.

> *"What's that for?"*
>
> *"So we know we did it."*

Tikat shows the Punic farmers that the barley they brought from the Levant will not grow here and
that the land wants something else. They learn from the people whose rent they are paying.

## Ends on

The cord on the wall. One knot. **Wordless, and the last page of Arc 1.**

## The cord — production standing order

This is the series' single continuous physical object (`story-bible.md` §10, `lore.md`).

| Property | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A length of ship's cable — **from the eight ships**, so it is physically part of the flight | `PROPOSED` |
| Length | Roughly an arm's length by Hannibal's day | `[F]` canon |
| Knots | One per generation. **One knot in this chapter.** Two by ch. 22 | `[F]` canon |
| Where it lives | **Hung on a wall**, Books One–Two. Not worn, not carried, until later | `PROPOSED` |
| Who ties | The head of the house of Yatonbaal, each generation | `PROPOSED` |
| Final fate | Given to Sosylos in the last chapter; burned. **Stated in narration, ch. 184** | `[F]` canon |

**A reference sheet for the cord is required before this chapter is drawn**, showing it at one
knot, and the same rope at two, five and twenty knots for later Books. It ages, frays and darkens,
and it must age consistently across six hundred years of story and ten years of production.
**Treat it as a character, not a prop.**

## Reader takeaway

1. They survived the year, and one of them decided that was worth recording.
2. The record is illegible to anyone outside the family, and will be for six centuries.
3. The settlers are being taught how to farm by the people they pay rent to.

## Information withheld

- That the cord matters. **No emphasis, no special framing, no narration box.** It is a man doing something small at the end of a hard winter. The series spends 190 chapters making it matter; this chapter must not try.
- That it burns. Ch. 184.

## The six words

> *"What's that for?"*
> *"So we know we did it."*

**Do not alter this dialogue.** It is `CANON-SOURCE`, verbatim from `THE-STORY.md`, and it is
the origin line of the series' central object.

Staging, `PROPOSED`: he does not look up while tying it — consistent with `CHR-34` throughout —
and looks up only on *"So we know we did it."* **That is the one time in Book One he looks directly
at his daughter**, and it should be the only panel in which he does.

## Tikat and the barley

Five pages, and it carries a theme on its own: **the settlers cannot feed themselves, and the
people they are paying rent to can.** Within sixty years they will be teaching the world how to
farm (Mago's twenty-eight books, Book Two); within two hundred they will own the teachers.

Play it as practical instruction, not as a lesson in humility. Tikat is not being generous — the
tenants dying would be inconvenient for the landlords.

## Craft notes

- **After ch. 9's blacks, this chapter is bright and empty.** The tonal recovery is the only reassurance Arc 1 offers, and it is deliberately modest.
- **The cord panel is small.** No full page, no dramatic lighting, no close-up push. It is on a wall in a room, and the camera treats it as furniture. **Its final appearance, 190 chapters later, gets the full page.**
- Storyboard with **ch. 9** as a pair, and with **ch. 22** (the second knot) as a matched composition — same wall, same angle, one more knot, twenty years.

## Arc 1 ends here

Ten chapters, 248 pages. A hundred and forty people left an island they could not return to and
became tenants on a hill that had no name. **Nobody has said the word "Carthage" yet** — that is
ch. 11, and the first thing anyone says about the name is an insult.

# Chapter 06 — EIGHTY

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Source: `THE-STORY.md` §5–6 · `arcs.md` Arc 1, marked ★.
Book One · Arc 1 · 26 pages.

> **This is the hardest chapter in the arc to make well and the easiest to make badly.**
> Read `style-guide.md` §12.4 and `CHR-43` before storyboarding a single panel.

## Role in the story

**The series' first statement that this city is not going to be clean.**

The sources are brisk: eighty women were taken from the shore so that the men would have wives,
because the city needed children. The series' whole position is in one line of the source —
*what is invented is that anyone bothered to ask what it was like.*

Chapter 4 made the audience admire Elissa. Chapter 6 exists so that admiration costs something. The
two chapters are only two apart for that reason.

## Summary

The boats come in. Eighty women are taken off the Cypriot shore. It is filmed from the shore, from
theirs — the boats arriving, the running, and a nineteen-year-old named Bititt who is carrying a
basket of someone else's washing and who is on a ship by evening.

She will get fourteen chapters: she marries a rope-maker's cousin, buries two children, teaches her
surviving son Cypriot Greek, and dies in ch. 20 of an infected tooth, having never once said she
forgave anybody.

## Opens on

An ordinary morning. Washing. The basket. **Six pages of nothing happening**, establishing a life
in enough detail that its interruption is felt as a loss rather than an event.

## Ends on

Bititt on a deck at evening, the basket still in her hands, looking back at a shore.
**She is not crying and the panel does not want her to.**

## Reader takeaway

1. The founding of this city included this.
2. It was ordinary. Nobody involved thought they were doing something historic.
3. Nobody stopped it, including the two women the audience likes.

## Information withheld

- Any judgement. **No narration box condemns it and none excuses it.** The chapter reports.
- Bititt's future. That she gets fourteen chapters is not signalled; she is introduced as one of eighty.

## Rules for this chapter — non-negotiable

Drawn from `style-guide.md` §12 and `CHR-43`:

1. **Filmed from the shore, from theirs.** Never a shot from the boats coming in. Never a Carthaginian eyeline. The camera is standing on the beach with the women.
2. **No fan-service framing of any kind.** No torn clothing as visual interest, no staged dishevelment, no low angles on bodies. Violence to a person is drawn as violence to a person.
3. **No sexual assault depicted.** The chapter ends at the deck. What the sources imply is not staged, and the series does not need it staged to be honest about it.
4. **The men are not monsters and are not absolved.** Ordinary faces, ordinary work. Mostly seen at middle distance and from behind.
5. **No music-video montage.** No slow-motion, no rain, no symbolic imagery. Flat, documentary, continuous.
6. **Elissa does not intervene, and is not given a conflicted reaction shot.** One panel. She sees it. The chapter moves on. **Giving her a flicker of remorse would be the single worst decision available in this chapter** — it would let the audience off, and it would be a lie about who she is.

## The basket

She is holding it when she is taken and it is still aboard in ch. 7. `CHR-43` proposes she keeps it
for fourteen chapters and it becomes what her son inherits.

**It is the chapter's one piece of deliberate composition** — an object that means nothing and is
carried anyway — and it is the counterweight to the knotted cord. One family records its survival
on purpose; another carries someone else's laundry for fourteen chapters because there was no
moment to put it down.

**Author's confirmation wanted** before it is written into scripts, because it commits Bititt's
whole arc to one image.

## Page budget

**26 pages.**

## Craft notes

- **The six-page opening is the chapter.** If the ordinary morning is not genuinely pleasant to read, nothing after it lands. Spend the pages.
- **No SFX during the taking.** The series' sound-effect budget is tiny and using it here would make the scene exciting. It must not be exciting.
- **Bititt looks directly at people** (`CHR-43` do-not-drift §4). Establish it in the first six pages so that the last panel — her looking back at the shore — is a behaviour the reader already knows.
- The chapter is a matched pair with **ch. 4**: both are about a group of people being made into something by a decision they did not take part in. **Do not stage them similarly.** Ch. 4 is operatic and dark; ch. 6 is flat and bright, and that contrast is the argument.

## Open question

**Do we hear what the women say to each other on the deck?** The source is silent. Proposal: yes,
briefly, in **untranslated Cypriot Greek** with no subtitle — the reader is in the same position as
the crew, which is the chapter's whole method. This also seeds `CHR-43` teaching her son that
language fourteen chapters later. Confirm.

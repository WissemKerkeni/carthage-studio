# Chapter 07 — THE COAST

**Status:** `PROPOSED`. Source: `THE-STORY.md` §7–9 · `arcs.md` Arc 1.
Book One · Arc 1 · 20 pages.

## Role in the story

Two corrections, delivered without argument:

1. **Africa is not empty.** *"This surprised nobody except later storytellers."* The land they are about to settle belongs to people who are already on it, farming it and grazing it, and the series says so before it says anything else about Africa.
2. **The neighbours will not help.** Utica is three hours up the coast, Phoenician, older, established, and entirely uninterested. The first Carthaginian foreign-policy experience is a polite no.

This chapter is where the audience is handed the fact that Book Two's entire moral architecture
rests on: **Carthage is a tenant.**

## Summary

The African coast, after the crossing. They are looking for somewhere to stop. They find a bay with
a lagoon behind it, a hill above it, and a long shallow shelf where ships can be drawn up — and it
belongs to people.

Up the coast, Utica. Old, Phoenician, successful, and politely unwilling to host eight ships of
refugees with no cargo manifest and an unclear legal status. Elissa is received courteously and
given nothing.

She comes back down the coast and starts working out who actually owns the hill.

## Opens on

Coastline from the sea, scrolling past — three pages of looking for somewhere, which establishes
that this was not a destination. **They did not sail to Africa. They ran out of alternatives.**

## Ends on

The hill, from the water, at the end of the day. Empty. **The single most important establishing
shot in Book One**, because the audience will see this ground again in every later Book with a city
on it.

## LOC-18 — build this carefully

This ground becomes Carthage. The audience sees it empty here, sees it built on across Arc 2, and
sees it as the greatest city in the western Mediterranean for the next six Books — and finally
watches it burn in ch. 195–200.

**Its establishing angles must be fixed now, while there is nothing on it**, so that every later
establishing shot of Carthage is the same camera position with six hundred years of construction
added. That recognition is worth more than any individual panel in this chapter.

Proposed fixed angles, `PROPOSED`:

1. **From the water, low** — the shelf, the lagoon behind, the hill above. The Book One signature.
2. **From the hilltop, looking seaward** — what a person standing on the Byrsa sees.
3. **From inland, looking north** — the approach that matters in Arc 5 and in Book Eight.

## Reader takeaway

1. The land is occupied and farmed. There is no empty continent.
2. Utica is Phoenician and will not help, and that is a decision Utica keeps making for six hundred years.
3. There is a specific hill above a specific bay, and the audience now knows what it looks like.

## Information withheld

- That this becomes Carthage. **Not stated, not narrated, not hinted.** The chapter ends on an empty hill and the reader is allowed to work it out.
- The rent. The negotiation is ch. 8 and nothing here previews its terms.

## Utica

Five pages. The scene is not hostile — that is the point. Courteous, generous with wine, careful
with commitments, and entirely immovable. The Utican magistrate is right on every point he makes:
these people have no standing, no cargo, no king, and a story about their treasure that does not
hold up.

**Play him as reasonable.** `story-bible.md` §9.3 in miniature — the arc's only real refusal comes
from someone with a good case. Utica's contempt becomes foreign policy in ch. 14 and gets Utica's
Carthaginian garrison massacred in Book Four; this is where that starts.

## The purple

Colour-arc step 4 of 5. **Undyed wool from here on** (`CHR-03` wardrobe: Africa, ch. 8–17). The
robe comes off in this chapter — proposal: she changes before going to Utica, and the reader should
notice that she chose to arrive looking poor rather than looking like a claimant. She keeps a
single gold pin from it.

## Page budget

**20 pages.** Roughly: 3 (coastline) + 4 (the bay found) + 5 (Utica) + 4 (the return) + 4 (the hill at evening).

## Craft notes

- **The Libyan herdsmen are seen before they are met**, at distance, twice, from the ships. The audience registers occupancy before the negotiation chapter makes it explicit.
- **No "we're home" beat.** Nobody is relieved. They have arrived somewhere they do not own, having been refused by the only people who share their language.
- The chapter is quiet and transitional, and that is correct after ch. 6. **Do not add incident.**

# CHR-36 — PUBLIUS CORNELIUS SCIPIO AEMILIANUS

## Identity
- **ID:** CHR-36 · **Name:** Publius Cornelius Scipio Aemilianus `[A]`; *Africanus the Younger* after 146
- **Role:** protagonist of Book Eight, with CHR-37
- **First appearance:** ch. 195 · **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **The descent — documented `[A]`, continuity-critical, and never to be treated as invented:**
> natural son of **L. Aemilius Paullus Macedonicus**; natural grandson of the **L. Aemilius Paullus
> killed at Cannae** (ch. 156); adopted into the Scipio house as the **grandson of Scipio Africanus**
> (CHR-04). The man who destroys Carthage is descended from the consul Hannibal annihilated *and*
> from the man who beat Hannibal. Two family lines that Hannibal broke meet in one body, outside the
> walls, seventy years later.

## Canonical description

A reserved, competent, slightly cold man of about thirty-six, built like his natural father and
carrying himself like his adoptive grandfather — which he is fully aware of and slightly uses. He is
the most professionally impressive person in Book Eight and the least warm: he keeps discipline
where three previous commanders lost it, he rescues other people's cut-off detachments, he never
raises his voice, and he does the thing he is sent to do without any of the appetite that would make
him easier to hate. He is a Hellenophile who reads Greek philosophy with a Greek prisoner and then
levels a city over the top of fifty thousand people. The series' whole case is that these are not
contradictions.

## Character

- **Want:** to finish, competently, a war that three commanders have bungled, and to be worthy of two
  names.
- **Need:** to know whether what he is doing is the same as what his grandfathers did, or something
  worse. He decides, in the last minutes of ch. 200, that it is the same — and that Rome will get it
  too.
- **Flaw:** he has no appetite for any of it and does it perfectly anyway, which is worse than
  cruelty and the series says so.
- **Fear:** named on screen, in the last scene, and it is not fear for himself.
- **Speech register:** dry, exact, unemphatic; gives orders in the fewest possible words; quotes
  Greek in private and never in public.
- **Body language:** still, watchful, physically brave without display. Touches nobody.

## Arc

Inherits, by birth and adoption, both halves of the war his family has been fighting for seventy
years, and is sent to end it by a Senate that has already decided. Does it faultlessly. Is given a
triumph and a name. And in the moment it is finished, standing next to a Greek prisoner, he becomes
the first Roman in the series to say out loud that the machine he serves will eventually do this to
his own city.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **He is not a sadist and not a reluctant hero.** He is a professional who has correctly understood
   his orders.
2. **Silhouette-match CHR-04 in the consul outfit.** The confusion is intentional and load-bearing.
3. **The tears in ch. 200 are real and public.** No stoic-mask staging. Polybius says he took hold of
   his hand; keep the hand.
4. **He never gloats and never speaks about Hannibal.** Hannibal is in every frame of Book Eight and
   in none of its dialogue.

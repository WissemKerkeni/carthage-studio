## Canonical description

A sinewy, deliberate man in his late thirties who has laid cord in the same four-hundred-pace lane
since he was nine, and whose entire professional life has been conducted with his back to where he
is going. He is not stupid and not cowed; he is careful in the specific way of a man whose trade
depends on tension being exactly right. When the second man in the city walks down his rope walk
alone and asks whether he can work in secret, he does not look up — and the series' second line of
dialogue tells the audience everything about how Tyre works: *"That depends on what you tell the
harbour master."*

He is better than most rope-makers and worse than his daughter, and he does not know it.

## Character

- **Want:** to keep the family's contract and the family's silence, in that order.
- **Need:** to see his daughter.
- **Flaw:** he answers a dangerous question instead of refusing it, and the family is aboard the ships because of it.
- **Fear:** the harbour master, the guild, and losing the lane — ordinary fears, which is the point.
- **Speech register:** dry, short, oblique. Answers questions with conditions. Never says yes.
- **Body language:** works while talking, always. Does not look at the person he is speaking to — not from rudeness, but because the rope will run out of true.

## Arc

Nine or ten chapters. He takes the commission, makes the rope that makes the escape possible, loses
the lane, the guild, the city and the contract in a single night, survives a winter that kills
three, and in the spring **ties a knot in a length of ship's cable and hangs it on a wall** —

> *"What's that for?"*
> *"So we know we did it."*

Two lines, and they are the origin of the object the series ends on. He has no idea what he has
started, which is the point. He dies offscreen and undramatically, and the cord passes to his
daughter because she is the one who is there.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **He never looks up while working.** In chapter 1 he does not meet Zakarbaal's eyes once.
2. **Grooming is the anti-Zakarbaal.** Cropped, unoiled, untended. The two men are a visual thesis.
3. **Crooked left fingers**, consistently, in every panel showing his hands.
4. **Not a wise mentor.** No speeches, no life lessons, no deathbed wisdom.

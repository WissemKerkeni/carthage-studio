# CHR-01 — HANNIBAL BARCA

## Identity
- **ID:** CHR-01
- **Name:** Hannibal Barca `[A]` — Punic *Ḥannibaʿl*, "grace of Baal"; *Barca* / *Baraq*, "lightning," the family name
- **Also called:** "the boy" (by Hamilcar's officers, ch. 115–128); *the one-eyed* (by his own troops, after ch. 149); *Hannibal ad portas* (by Romans, from ch. 158)
- **Role:** protagonist (central from ch. 134; present from ch. 115)
- **First appearance:** ch. 115, as a child of nine
- **Status:** `PROPOSED`

## Canonical description

A compact, dark, deeply weathered man who does not look like a commander and takes some trouble
not to. Mediterranean-dark skin burned darker by twenty years outdoors; black hair, cut short and
badly, going grey at the temples by Cannae and fully grey by Zama; a short beard he shaves off in
the field because of lice. Hooded, very steady eyes — the right one lost to infection in ch. 149,
after which the socket is closed and slightly sunken and he turns his whole head to look at people,
which reads as scrutiny and eventually terrifies everyone. Dresses worse than his officers, on
purpose: an ordinary Iberian tunic, a plain cloak, good boots, and the only expensive thing on him
is his horse. Polybius records that he ate and slept on the ground among the men, wrapped in a
military cloak `[A]`, and the art must hold that line: **he is never the best-dressed person in the
panel.**

## Character

- **Want:** to break Rome's hold on Italy and force a negotiated peace that restores Carthage's
  position. *Not* to destroy Rome — the series is explicit that he never had the means or,
  probably, the intention.
- **Need:** to find out whether the oath is his own will or his father's. He never does.
- **Flaw:** he believes every problem is an engineering problem. Given a state that will not
  behave like an army, he has nothing.
- **Fear:** being one of his father's instruments rather than a man. Manifests as a horror of being
  *handled* — which is why ch. 116's cut to the waiting officers is unbearable.
- **Speech register:** short declaratives. Almost never raises his voice; when he does, it is
  logistics, not morale. Asks questions instead of giving orders where possible. **Never makes a
  speech about hatred of Rome on screen** — other people make those speeches about him. Dry, very
  occasional humour, always at the expense of grandeur (see the Antiochus review, ch. 180).
- **Body language:** stillness. Stands with weight even on both feet; does not gesture; turns the
  whole head after ch. 149. Eats standing. Sleeps anywhere, instantly, which unsettles people.

## Arc

**Starts** as a nine-year-old who wants his father to take him somewhere, and is given a religious
oath instead of an answer.

**Becomes**, over nineteen chapters, exactly the instrument that was required: the best logistician
and tactician of the ancient world, raised in a camp, fluent in the languages of the men he will
spend.

**Breaks** three times, each time quieter than the last: at ch. 157, when Rome refuses to behave
like a defeated state and he realises there is no next move; at ch. 168, when he opens the sack; and
at ch. 175, when his own veterans, the men from Italy, are ridden down from behind by cavalry of the
kind he used to have.

**Ends** as a competent, unremarkable political exile, advising second-rate kings, in a rented house
with seven exits cut into the walls — having, in the one year he was actually allowed to govern his
own city, done more lasting good with an audit than with sixteen years of victories.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **No eyepatch.** The right eye is closed and sunken, not covered. Artists add eyepatches
   automatically; reject every generation that does.
2. **He is short and under-dressed.** Never the tallest, never the most decorated figure in a group
   shot. If he reads as "the hero" at a glance, the panel is wrong.
3. **No elephants after ch. 148** except at Zama, and at Zama they are small, mangy and untrained —
   forest elephants, ~2.5 m, **no towers**.
4. **Age the face, not the hair only.** Between ch. 137 and ch. 175 he goes from 29 to 45 in a
   climate and a profession that ages men fast.
5. **He is never smiling in a battle panel.** He smiles exactly four times in Book Five, all of them
   off the battlefield.

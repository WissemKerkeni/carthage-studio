## Identity

- **ID:** CHR-33 · **Name:** Zakarbaal `[C]` — Greek sources call him **Acerbas**, Latin **Sychaeus**. On screen, always Zakarbaal
- **Titles:** priest of Melqart; "the second man in the city"
- **Role:** the inciting figure. Husband of Elissa; murdered by Pygmalion
- **First appearance:** ch. 1 · **Murdered:** ch. 3, off screen · **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Evidence note.** `[C]` — attested only in the foundation legend, and his name varies by source.
> The series keeps the murder and, per `story/THE-STORY.md`, makes the motive **fiscal rather than
> simply greedy**: the temple was refusing to fund the Assyrian tribute and the king could not be
> king without it. He is never shown alive after ch. 2.

## Canonical description

A careful, orderly, middle-aged priest who has spent his whole life being the second most powerful
man in a rich city and has never once been frightened — and who is, when we meet him, frightened,
and doing it badly. He is not a schemer. He is an administrator improvising a conspiracy, and the
audience should be able to see the improvisation: he comes to the rope walk alone, which is
conspicuous, and asks a question that tells a stranger exactly what he is planning.

His competence is real but wrongly shaped. He can run a temple treasury against Assyrian auditors.
He cannot run an escape.

## Character

- **Want:** to get his wife and his faction out of Tyre alive, without anyone noticing.
- **Need:** to understand that he is already too late.
- **Flaw:** he thinks secrecy is a thing money buys rather than a thing behaviour maintains.
- **Fear:** Pygmalion — specifically, being *audited* by him.
- **Speech register:** precise, administrative, courteous, and far too direct for a conspirator. Asks for exactly what he wants. His first line in the series is a question no careful man would ask a stranger.
- **Body language:** still, upright, hands clasped. Fastidious — the hem, the tar, the distance kept from the work.

## Arc

Three chapters. He is introduced doing something quiet and slightly wrong, is explained in chapter 2, and is killed off screen in chapter 3 — the audience learning it at the same moment and in the
same room as Elissa. His function is to be the debt that starts everything: the stolen treasure is
his, the flight is his plan executed by someone better at it, and the city that results is built on
his money and his failure.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Soft hands, no callus.** Set against Elishat's in chapter 1. This contrast is the chapter's whole visual argument.
2. **Not sinister.** No shadowed eyes, no scheming smile. He is a worried accountant.
3. **Dressed down but obviously rich.** The cloth is plain and the quality is unmistakable.
4. **Never shown alive after ch. 2**, and never shown dead. The murder stays off screen.
5. **Oiled, formally curled hair and beard** — he is the series' first example of Punic elite grooming, and later Carthaginian elites are drawn against this template.

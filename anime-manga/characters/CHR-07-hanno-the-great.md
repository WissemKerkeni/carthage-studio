# CHR-07 — HANNO II, "THE GREAT"

## Identity
- **ID:** CHR-07 · **Name:** Hanno, called the Great `[A]` · **Role:** principal political antagonist,
  Books Three–Five · **First appearance:** ch. 66 · **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Standing instruction from the story bible:** *he is right more often than the audience wants
> him to be.* Any script that makes him a coward, a miser or a traitor is rejected.

## Canonical description

A large, soft, immaculately dressed man in his sixties who has never been popular and has never
needed to be. He is the voice of the Carthaginian merchant oligarchy: that Carthage is a trading
state whose wealth comes from Africa and the sea lanes, that land empires are ruinously expensive,
that armies loyal to families become monarchies, and that a war with Rome is a war the city cannot
pay for. He is wrong about Hamilcar Barca's competence and right about everything Hamilcar's
competence leads to. He also commands badly, at Utica in 240 BC, and men die for it, and the series
shows that too.

## Character

- **Want:** to keep the constitution intact and the balance sheet solvent.
- **Need:** to be believed before the disaster rather than after it. He never is.
- **Flaw:** he is correct in a register nobody wants to hear, and he enjoys being correct, which
  costs him every vote he should have won.
- **Fear:** Bomilcar. Explicitly, by name, twice. He was a young man when they crucified him in the
  marketplace, and he has never stopped drawing the obvious conclusion.
- **Speech register:** parliamentary, numerate, faintly contemptuous. Deals in figures. His speeches
  are the only place the audience gets Carthage's actual accounts, which is why they cannot be cut.
- **Body language:** sits. Waits. Lets other people finish and then reads out a number.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Never sneering in the panel.** He is reasonable, tired, and usually right.
2. **He is the only recurring character who wears purple.** Colour-lock it.
3. Ch. 177: his congratulation is **sincere**, and the letter to Rome is also sincere. Both.

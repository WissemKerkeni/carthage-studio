# CHR-37 — HASDRUBAL THE BOETHARCH

## Identity
- **ID:** CHR-37 · **Name:** Hasdrubal `[A]`, called the **Boetharch** (*the relief-commander*) —
  the last general of Carthage
- **Role:** co-protagonist of Book Eight · **First appearance:** ch. 189 · **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Evidence note.** He is real `[A]`: commander at Oroscopa in 150 BC, condemned to death in
> absentia by his own government to appease Rome, recalled to command the field army in 149,
> responsible for the beating-to-death of a colleague in the senate house and for the mutilation of
> Roman prisoners on the walls, and he surrendered to Scipio at the end and was kept alive in Italy.
> His birth year, appearance and private life are `[F]`.

## Canonical description

A big, handsome, theatrical man in his late forties who wears purple in a city that is starving,
keeps a bodyguard among people who have nothing, and gives speeches on walls. He has read everything
there is to read about Hannibal Barca and has absorbed the posture without the arithmetic: he
understands that a great commander is feared, and has not understood that Hannibal was feared
because of what he knew about ground, supply and other men's temperaments. He is brave. He is not
a coward at any point, including at the end — what he does at the end is worse than cowardice, which
is that he calculates correctly, for himself, one time.

## Character

- **Want:** to be Hannibal.
- **Need:** to be worth following. He never works out that these are different things.
- **Flaw:** he confuses being feared with being obeyed, and terror with strategy. Every atrocity he
  commits is aimed at his own side.
- **Fear:** that the city will negotiate around him and hand him over — which is precisely what
  happened to him once already, in ch. 189, and is the reason for everything he does afterwards.
- **Speech register:** rhetorical, loud, public. Speaks in the third person about himself twice.
  **Never says anything true in a room with more than two people in it.**
- **Body language:** performs. Stands on things. Looks at the crowd before he answers.

## Arc

A competent second-rate soldier is beaten, disowned by his own city and sentenced to death; is
recalled when there is no one else; and spends three years making it impossible for anyone around
him to do anything except fight to the end — and then, at the end, does the one thing he has spent
three years killing people for proposing.

He is kept alive in Italy, in comfortable custody, for the rest of his life. **The series does not
show it.** Ch. 200 leaves him on his knees, and the audience learns what happened to him from a
single line of Polybius' closing narration.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **He is not a buffoon.** He is brave, capable, and completely wrong about what command is.
2. **The clean purple cloak in a starving city** is his entire design. Never let the art dirty it
   before ch. 200.
3. **Physically the anti-Hannibal:** tall, handsome, loud, decorated, unwounded, watched. Hold every
   axis.
4. He is **not** the villain of Book Eight. There is no villain of Book Eight. Rome voted for the
   war before he was recalled.

# CHR-12 — ELISHAT

## Identity

- **ID:** CHR-12 · **Name:** Elishat, of the house of Yatonbaal `[F]`
- **Role:** ground-level point of view, Book One. **The audience's eyes in chapter 1.**
- **First appearance:** ch. 1 (age 14) · **Last appearance:** ch. 21–22 (age ~55, grey)
- **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Function.** She is the first Knot-keeper the audience meets, and the series' whole six-hundred-year
> structure rests on her: her father ties the first knot in the cord, she becomes its keeper, and it
> ends on Sosylos' table in ch. 184. She is invented `[F]`, carries no plot power, and changes no
> historical outcome. She witnesses, splices, and survives.

## Canonical description

A small, wiry fourteen-year-old with a rope-maker's hands and an adult's watchfulness, who is better
at her trade than her father and has not been told so. She listens more than she speaks, and the
series uses her stillness as its camera: when Elishat looks at something, the reader is being told
it matters. She is not precocious, not spirited, not a plucky audience-surrogate child. She is a
working child in a working family, tired most of the time, and interested in ropes.

Across Book One she grows from 14 to about 55 without ever becoming important. That is the point:
the series' longest-running figure is a person history would not record.

## Character

- **Want:** to be allowed to do the work she is already better at than her father.
- **Need:** to be recorded. She spends her life making the object that records her family, and the series' last line tells the audience it burned.
- **Flaw:** she does not speak when speaking would change something. Four times in Book One she sees clearly and says nothing — including at the pyre.
- **Fear:** being sent away from the rope.
- **Speech register:** flat, short, concrete, trade-specific. Talks about lengths, weights and lays. Never abstract. Her longest speech in Book One is eleven words.
- **Body language:** still, hands always busy. Watches people's hands rather than their faces — a tic the art department must hold, because it is how her panels are composed.

## Arc

Begins as a child splicing rope in the safest rich city in the world. Is aboard the escape because
somebody has to splice the rigging — the series' whole method in one line. Watches a hundred and
forty people become a single thing, watches her father tie the first knot of a record that will
outlast everyone in it, and ends Book One as a grey woman at the back of a crowd listening to a
priest tell children a version of her own life that is better than what happened, saying nothing.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Plain, and drawn plain.** No prettiness pass. She is never the most attractive figure in a panel.
2. **The hands.** Callus, tar, and disproportionate strength. If the hands are generic, the panel is rejected.
3. **She is not spirited.** No defiant chin, no clenched fists, no spunky-orphan energy. She is tired.
4. **She watches hands, not faces.** Composition follows her eyeline to other people's hands.
5. **Short hair until ch. 15**, then covered. Never loose and flowing.

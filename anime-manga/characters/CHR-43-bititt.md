## Identity

- **First appearance:** ch. 6 (age 19) · **Dies:** ch. 20, of an infected tooth
- **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Evidence note.** The eighty women are in the sources, which are brisk about them: taken from the
> shore so that the men would have wives, because the city needed children. **Bititt is invented.**
> What is invented is not the event — it is that anyone bothered to ask what it was like.

## Canonical description

A nineteen-year-old Cypriot woman who was carrying a basket of someone else's washing when the boats
came in, and who was on a ship by evening. She is not a victim in the sentimental sense and not a
survivor in the inspirational one. She is a person to whom something was done, who then lived for
fourteen more chapters in the city that did it, married into it, raised a son in it, and never at
any point said she forgave anybody.

The series does not give her a revenge, a reconciliation, or a redemptive moment. It gives her
fourteen chapters of ordinary life, which is harder to watch and is the point.

## Character

- **Want:** to go home. This never becomes anything more sophisticated, and it is never granted.
- **Need:** nothing the story provides.
- **Flaw:** none that causes the plot. She is not the author of her situation.
- **Fear:** that her son will be a Carthaginian, which he is.
- **Speech register:** Cypriot Greek first, then broken Punic, then fluent Punic with an accent nobody stops noticing. **She teaches her son Cypriot Greek** — the first non-Punic language transmitted inside Carthage, and a quiet setup for a city that will always be full of other people's languages.
- **Body language:** economical, watchful. Does not flinch and does not soften.

## Arc

Taken in chapter 6. Alive in chapter 20. Between those, she does what the eighty women did: she
builds the city's next generation, without consent and without commentary. Her death is an infected
tooth — not tragedy, not punishment, just the ordinary way people died — and it lands in the middle
of Arc 2 without ceremony.

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Never framed attractively.** No fan-service, no dishevelled-beauty staging. `style-guide.md` §12.4 governs ch. 6 absolutely.
2. **The taking is filmed from the shore, from hers.** Never from the boats, never from a Carthaginian eyeline.
3. **She never forgives and is never asked to.** No reconciliation scene. No deathbed absolution.
4. **She looks directly at people.** Hold this; it is her one distinguishing behaviour.
5. **Her hands are laundry hands**, not rope hands. The Book One hand-vocabulary depends on them being different.

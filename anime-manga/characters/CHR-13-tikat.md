# CHR-13 — TIKAT

## Identity

- **ID:** CHR-13 · **Name:** Tikat `[F]`, of the tent of Iyarut
- **Role:** Libyan POV, Book One. **POV of ch. 8 — the most important chapter in Book One**
- **First appearance:** ch. 8 (age 20) · **Recurs:** ch. 10, 13, and through Arc 2
- **Status:** `PROPOSED`

> **Function.** She is the first of the **tent of Iyarut** `[F]`, one of the series' four continuity
> lineages — *Carthage seen by the people it taxes* (`story-bible.md` §10). The line runs from
> landlords of the first city, through the Truceless War, to Masinissa's cavalry at Zama, to the
> family above the Bagradas in the series' final image. Everything that line means starts with her,
> in a tent, watching foreigners count.
>
> She is invented, carries no plot power, and changes no historical outcome. The **rent itself is
> `[A]`** — documented, and the hinge of the whole series.

## Canonical description

A twenty-year-old Libyan woman, numerate, unhurried, and present at the founding negotiation
because her father wanted someone who could count to be watching the foreigners count. She is not
fooled by anything in chapter 8 and she is outmatched anyway — not on arithmetic, where she is her
father's equal and Elissa's near-equal, but on a point of concept. Her family is selling access to
land. Elissa is buying a permanent claim on it. Both parties leave the tent believing they agreed
the same thing, and only one of them is right.

She is the series' counter-protagonist in the structural sense: everything Carthage achieves for
six hundred years is a bill her descendants pay.

## Character

- **Want:** to know exactly what her father has agreed to.
- **Need:** a word for a thing her language does not have — permanent alienable title in land.
- **Flaw:** she trusts arithmetic to be the whole of an agreement. It is not; the concepts underneath it are, and that is where her family loses.
- **Fear:** nothing in Book One. **She is not afraid of the newcomers and never becomes afraid of them**, which is a deliberate contrast with every Punic character's fear of Pygmalion, Assyria, or the sea.
- **Speech register:** plain, concrete, slightly formal. Repeats a term back before agreeing to it — a negotiator's habit, and the source of at least one of chapter 8's mistranslations.
- **Body language:** still, economical. **Watches the tally, not the speakers** — a compositional rule for ch. 8, and a deliberate pairing with Elishat, who watches hands.

## Arc

Present at the founding agreement as an observer with a ledger. Returns in ch. 10 to teach the
settlers that their Levantine barley will not grow here — practical instruction, not generosity;
the tenants dying would be inconvenient. Carries the memory of what was actually agreed into a
family that will still be repeating it when Carthage stops paying in ch. 28, two hundred pages later.

What she remembers is not the bargaining. It is that at the end of eleven days the foreign queen
asked, through two interpreters, badly, what the hill was called — **and nobody had ever asked. It
was the hill. It was called the hill.**

## Do-not-drift list

1. **Not a noble savage, and not a victim.** She is numerate, well-dressed, well-fed and unafraid. Her family are landlords.
2. **Not fooled.** She never misses a number. What she misses is a concept, and the chapter must make that distinction visible or the theme collapses into condescension.
3. **Silver, never gold.** Holds for the whole tent of Iyarut lineage, six hundred years.
4. **Ochre-dressed braids**, researched and consistent. The series' first non-Punic hair vocabulary.
5. **She watches the tally, not the speakers.** Compositional rule for ch. 8.
6. **No romance with any Punic character**, in this Book or any other.
